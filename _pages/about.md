I am a Ph.D. student in Political Science at Purdue University, where my major field is International Relations (International Political Economy) and my minor field is Comparative Politics.

My research sits at the intersection of international political economy, science and technology policy, and legislative behavior. One line of work examines who legislates for science in the United States: using a large language model, I measure fifty years of congressional science policy and study how local economic interests shape restrictive science legislation in an era of strategic competition. A second line asks how rising powers pursue status, focusing on how governments communicate prestige projects to domestic audiences. I also work on the measurement of digital trade and on how social context shapes the expression of political attitudes.

Methodologically, I combine computational text analysis and LLM-based measurement with survey experiments and multilevel models, and I am interested in how to validate LLM annotation as a measurement tool in the social sciences.

Before Purdue, I earned an M.P.A. from Brown University's Watson Institute and a B.A. in International Studies from UC San Diego, and I worked on higher education policy at UNESCO-ICHEI and on climate policy at the World Resources Institute. At Purdue, I teach POL 327 Global Green Politics as instructor of record and serve as a lab instructor for the graduate quantitative methods sequence.
